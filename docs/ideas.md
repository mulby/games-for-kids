# Game ideas from the overnight brainstorm (2026-10-08)

Three designers brainstormed 24 ideas, each through a different lens: pretend play, hands-on manipulatives, and adventure/collecting. A judge scored them on replay appeal, how well the math is woven into the play, fit for a struggling near-1st-grade player, and feasibility. Ideas that overlapped the three games other threads were building (Egypt, mouse shop, pirates/gods) were set aside for this round.

## All ideas

### Honeycake Bakery: Ten-Tray Boxes
She runs a cozy Maileg-style mouse bakery where every order goes into bakery boxes shaped like ten-frames. Making ten is how you pack a box, so filling boxes and tying ribbons is the addition practice.

*Skills:* Counting to 20 with one-to-one correspondence; teen numbers as 10 + some; number bonds to 10 ('how many more fill the box?'); addition within 20 by making ten (8+5 = 10+3); doubles (twin orders); counting on.

### The Store Stump: Brambly Hedge Pantry
She is keeper of a hedgerow mouse storehouse inside an old tree stump. Nuts, berries and seeds come in jars of exactly ten plus loose ones, so fetching every order is tens-and-ones practice she holds in her hands.

*Skills:* Counting and reading numbers to 120; tens and ones place value; bundling 10 loose ones into a ten; comparing 2-digit numbers; later 2-digit addition (and some subtraction) with regrouping shown as filling a new jar.

### The Thimble Shop: Tiny Furniture Store
She runs a little shop selling dollhouse furniture to mice and bunnies. Customers pay with coins she counts into the till, and she spends what she earns on furniture for her own mouse house, so there are two kinds of coin practice and both feel like play.

*Skills:* Coin recognition (1¢, 5¢, 10¢, 25¢ drawn as simple generic coins with big value labels); counting pennies to 20; counting dimes by 10s; dimes and pennies as tens and ones; counting nickels by 5s; mixed counting up from the largest coin; comparing amounts to a price ('enough?'); making an exact amount.

### The Moonlit Inn: A Hotel for Sleepy Creatures
She runs a cozy inn where magical creatures check in and ask to be woken at certain times. Setting each room's clock is the job, and the hotel wakes up because she set it.

*Skills:* Telling time to the hour and half hour on an analog clock; matching analog to digital (3:00, 3:30); understanding 'half past' as the minute hand on half of the clock face (links to halves); ordering events in time along a timeline; telling when one hour later.

### The Fairy Tea Party: Fair Shares
She hosts tea parties for fairies, and her job is to make sure every guest gets a fair share. She slices cakes, pies and sandwiches with her finger and shares berries evenly, so halves, fourths and equal sharing are the whole party.

*Skills:* Equal vs. unequal parts; halves and fourths of circles, rectangles and squares; naming 'half' and 'fourth' (quarter); seeing that 2 halves and 4 fourths each make a whole; equal sharing of small sets (8 berries for 4 fairies), an early form of equal groups and division; optional logic: a 4x4 table-setting puzzle (Latin square).

### Olympus Potion Shop
She is the apprentice potion-maker on Mount Olympus, brewing potions for the Greek gods by adding scoops of ingredients that come in groups of 2, 5 and 10. A cauldron gauge shaped like a number line makes skip counting something she can see and pour.

*Skills:* Skip counting by 2s, 5s and 10s (to 50, later to 100 and 120); equal groups ('3 groups of 5'); repeated addition; predicting the next number in a skip-count; equal sharing into bottles; counting on from a skip-count.

### Temple Cat Clinic of the Nile
She is the caretaker at an Egyptian temple clinic for cats. Every patient has a number on its collar and must be carried down a long hall of numbered baskets, so placing cats on the number line is the job, and comparing which cat needs more is the care.

*Skills:* Number sense to 20, then to 100 and 120, on a linear number line; placing numbers between landmarks (0, 10, 20... with half-way 5s); comparing two numbers (more, fewer, bigger, smaller); ordering 3 numbers; one more and one less, ten more and ten less (stretch).

### Captain Pip's Ship Galley
She is the cook on a pirate mouse ship, feeding a hungry crew from biscuit barrels shaped like ten-frames. Subtraction is serving the crew, and 'how many more do we bake?' is the missing-number puzzle.

*Skills:* Subtraction within 10, then within 20 (take away, counting back); missing addends ('we have 8, we need 12, how many more?'); fact families (8 + 4 = 12 so 12 - 4 = 8); subtracting using 10 (14 - 4 = 10, then 10 - 2); stretch: 2-digit minus tens with barrels of 10.

### Hazelnut Pantry: Brambly Bundles
A cozy mouse-pantry storage game. She gathers loose hazelnuts and ties every 10 into a bundle with a ribbon, then stores the bundles and loose nuts on labeled pantry shelves. Tens and ones are things she physically ties and stacks. The pantry slowly fills up and becomes her dollhouse-style storeroom.

*Skills:* Counting to 120, grouping by tens, tens-and-ones place value (2-digit numbers as bundles plus loose ones), reading and building 2-digit numbers, later +10 and -10 (adding or taking a whole bundle) and comparing two orders.

### Fairy Lantern Festival
She fills glass fairy lanterns by dragging glowing fireflies onto ten-frames inside each lantern. A full ten-frame lights up the lantern, and the lanterns string together over a dark fairy glade until the whole festival is lit. The ten-frame is the lantern, so making ten is literally making light.

*Skills:* Composing 10 (number bonds to 10), 'make ten' strategy for adding within 20 (8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3), doubles and near-doubles, counting on, and later subtraction as 'fireflies flying home' (14 - 6).

### Captain Pip's Number-Line Voyage
A pirate sailing game on a linear sea-chart. Her little ship sits on a numbered sea-path and she moves it by dragging 'wind cards' (+3, +10, -2) or by tapping where a treasure island must be. The number line is the map, so adding is sailing forward and subtracting is sailing back.

*Skills:* Linear number sense 0-20, then 0-100 and to 120. Placing and estimating numbers on a number line, counting on and counting back, adding and subtracting within 20 as hops, jumps of 10 (+10/-10 on a hundred chart), early 2-digit addition by hopping tens then ones, and skip counting by 2s, 5s and 10s ('wind gusts').

### The Scales of Ma'at
An Egyptian temple puzzle with a golden balance scale. She drags stone blocks, scarab weights and number tiles onto each pan until the scale settles level and a hidden door slides open. Equality isn't a symbol to memorize: she sees and feels it as the scale tipping and evening out.

*Skills:* Comparing numbers (more, less, equal) to 20 then to 100, the meaning of the equals sign, missing addends (7 + _ = 10), equivalent sums (8 + 5 = 10 + 3), doubles (6 + 6 balancing 12), and later comparing 2-digit numbers by tens and ones.

### Rune Stones of Olympus (Sum Sudoku)
A small, friendly logic-puzzle game. She drags glowing rune stones into tiny grids (3x3 and 4x4) where each row has no repeats and colored 'cages' must add to a target number. It is easy sudoku plus KenKen, and every cage is an addition puzzle in disguise.

*Skills:* Addition within 10, then within 20, number bonds (which two stones make 5?), sums of three addends, logical reasoning, and later missing addends and simple subtraction ('difference' cages for older play).

### Thimble & Teacup Market
A coin-counting shop game for a tiny dollhouse. She shops at a mouse market for miniature furniture and decor, and pays by dragging coins onto the shop counter. Everything she buys goes into her mouse house, which she decorates however she likes.

*Skills:* Coin recognition and values (penny, nickel, dime, quarter, using generic drawn coins), counting by 1s, 5s and 10s (skip counting), counting mixed coins starting with the biggest, making amounts to 99 cents, and later more than one way to make the same amount.

### Honeycake Bakery: Fair Shares
A bakery game about cutting, sharing and equal groups. She slices cakes, pies and honey-buns with a swipe, then serves the pieces onto plates for woodland guests so everyone gets a fair share. Fractions and equal groups are something she cuts and hands out.

*Skills:* Halves and fourths (equal parts, naming 'half' and 'quarter/fourth'), recognizing equal vs. unequal parts, two halves or four fourths make a whole, early equal groups and sharing (12 berries shared among 3 plates), early skip counting by 2s and 5s, and 'how many in all' for equal groups.

### The Sleepy Owl's Clock Tower
A time-telling game where she sets a big, friendly clock tower by dragging the hands, and each hour wakes up a different part of the village day. Her pictures of the day line up along a timeline she can replay. Time isn't drilled: she moves the hands and sees the sun, moon and village change as she does.

*Skills:* Telling time to the hour and half hour on an analog clock, how the hour and minute hands move together, matching analog to digital (7:00, 7:30), sequencing daily events, and later skip counting by 5s around the clock and quarter hours.

### Acorn Lane Furniture Workshop
A tiny mouse carpentry shop in a hedgerow, inspired by Brambly Hedge and Maileg. Mouse families send orders like '34 acorn caps for Granny Bramble's new dresser'. She fills each order by dragging tied bundles of ten and loose ones onto a little wheelbarrow. Every finished order gives her a piece of miniature furniture to place in her own mouse dollhouse.

*Skills:* Tens and ones place value to 120, bundling ten ones into a ten, and reading 2-digit numbers. Later stages cover 2-digit addition by joining two orders (23 + 14 means adding tens to tens and ones to ones), regrouping (a 'Tie a bundle!' moment when she has 10 loose acorns), and subtracting tens and ones when a mouse returns part of an order.

### Starlight Hatchery: Creature Cards
An original creature-card collecting game. Glowing eggs need exactly the right number of star-sparks to hatch. She fills a double ten-frame nest with sparks, and the egg hatches into a collectible trading card with a number-power that goes into her binder.

*Skills:* Addition within 10, then within 20. The game focuses on making ten (8 + 5 means filling the first frame, then 3 more), doubles and near-doubles, counting on, and missing addends (the egg needs 12 and the nest already has 7). Composing and decomposing numbers to 20 runs through all of it.

### The Pharaoh's Rune Puzzles
Small, gentle sudoku and KenKen-style puzzles carved on the doors of an Egyptian tomb. Each solved door opens a treasure chamber, and its artifact goes into her own museum gallery with a little story placard.

*Skills:* Number sense to 4 through logical reasoning (4x4 sudoku with hieroglyph symbols that gradually become numerals 1 to 4). Addition within 10 with sum cages: 3x3 KenKen-style grids where cages show a target like '5' and she places numbers that add to it. Composing numbers (all the ways to make 6) and early part-part-whole reasoning.

### Captain Pip's Number Sea
A pirate voyage along a winding numbered sea-path. A friendly mouse captain needs her to steer the ship to the right number-islands. She taps or slides the ship to where a number lands on the line, hops by 2s, 5s and 10s, and digs up treasure for her pirate chest.

*Skills:* Linear number line estimation and placement 0 to 20, then 0 to 100 and 0 to 120. Counting forward and backward, 'one more / one less' and 'ten more / ten less', comparing which island is farther, and skip counting by 2s, 5s and 10s with 'Wind Gusts' that move the ship in equal hops.

### Scales of Olympus
Greek gods weigh offerings on golden balance scales. She makes the scales balance by placing or removing glowing orbs, and every balanced scale lights a star in a god's constellation. A finished constellation earns that god's collectible card and blessing.

*Skills:* Comparing numbers (more, less, equal) and the meaning of the equals sign as 'balanced'. Addition and subtraction within 10, then within 20. Missing addends ('7 + ? = 10'), take-away subtraction (remove orbs to balance), equations with expressions on both sides (8 + 5 = 10 + 3) and early comparison symbols (<, >).

### Fairy Tea Party Pantry
She hosts tea parties for fairy guests in Moonberry Garden. She shares cakes, pies and berries fairly by cutting them into halves and fourths and dealing treats into equal groups on tiny plates. Every successful party earns a piece of a fairy tea set and decorations for her own fairy tea room.

*Skills:* Halves and fourths of shapes (circles, rectangles) with equal parts, plus recognizing which cuts are fair and naming a half and a quarter. Early equal groups: dealing objects so each plate has the same number (sharing 8 berries among 2 or 4 guests), repeated addition (3 plates with 2 cakes each makes 2, 4, 6) and doubles.

### Hedgerow Clock Days
A day in the life of a mouse village. She sets the hands of the great Clocktower to the hour or half hour, and the village comes alive at that time: bakers open, children go to school, lanterns light up. Each correctly set time adds a scene to her illustrated storybook of the day.

*Skills:* Telling time to the hour and half hour on an analog clock and matching it to digital time. Understanding that the hour hand moves along between numbers at half past. Sequencing events in a day (morning, noon, evening), and before/after by an hour. Optional extension: counting by 5s around the clock face.

### Honeycake Bakery Coin Counter
She runs her own bakery stall in the Honeycake Bakery. Animal customers buy treats, and she counts their coins or makes exact change with pennies, nickels and dimes. Earnings buy decorations and new recipes for her bakery, which she designs and arranges herself.

*Skills:* Coin values (1, 5, 10, later 25), counting collections of coins by 1s, 5s and 10s, counting on from the largest coin, making a price with exact coins, comparing amounts (do I have enough?), and early subtraction for change within 20.
